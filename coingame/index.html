The strategy behind the benchmark

The Kelly criterion.

The reference strategy used the Kelly criterion, a formula for choosing the fraction of a portfolio to wager when the probabilities and payouts are known. Under this experiment's assumptions, the full Kelly stake is 20% of the current portfolio.

Stake fraction = probability of win − probability of loss
20% = 60% − 40%

A win earns the amount staked and a loss loses the amount staked. With a 60% chance of winning, the Kelly fraction is 0.60 − 0.40 = 0.20.

Betting too little can limit growth. Betting too much increases the damage caused by losses and can reduce long-term compound growth. Kelly maximises expected long-run logarithmic growth under the model's assumptions.

Kelly does not guarantee the highest final balance in 20 rounds. Random outcomes can make another strategy finish ahead. One short simulation is not proof that a strategy is superior.

Assumptions: independent flips, known 60% Heads probability, even-money payouts, and no fees or deposits.

In investing, you face two broad choices: aim to get the outcome right more often by risking a smaller amount each time, or take larger positions that can produce bigger gains and losses.

Style A · Smaller stakes

Win more often.

Risk a smaller share of your portfolio each time. This limits the impact of individual mistakes.

Style B · Larger stakes

Make each decision count.

Commit more of your portfolio to each view. Correct calls have more impact, but incorrect calls cost more.

The maths behind the reference

The Kelly criterion.

The reference strategy bets 20% of its current portfolio on Heads each round. With a 60% chance of winning and an even-money payout, the full Kelly fraction is:

60% − 40% = 20%

Kelly is designed to maximise expected long-run logarithmic growth when probabilities and payouts are known. It does not guarantee the highest balance over 20 rounds. Random outcomes can favour a different strategy.

This is an educational exercise, not a validated psychological assessment.
